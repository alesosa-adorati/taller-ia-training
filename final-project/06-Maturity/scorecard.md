# Kestrel AI Maturity Scorecard

Scores use a 1–5 scale with half-points. They are evidence-based assessments after discovery, not the initial prediction.

| Dimension | Score | Evidence | Gap to next level |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Leadership / Strategy | 3.0 | COO has explicit business priority and willingness to accept a no-buy conclusion. | Named AI/change owner and repeatable portfolio process |
| People / Skills | 2.5 | 51% of survey respondents reported AI use; only 6% reported training. | Role-specific training and safe-use practices |
| Data | 2.5 | Structured systems exist, but key knowledge sits in spreadsheets and personal workarounds. | Governed operational knowledge and workflow-level data ownership |
| Tools / Technology | 2.5 | APIs exist for several systems, but portals, fax and legacy databases remain material. | Bounded integration patterns and reusable approved components |
| Governance / Risk | 1.5 | No administrative AI policy; no general-purpose AI BAA. | Defined approval path, BAA, risk assessment, audit and human-review controls |
| Adoption / Usage | 2.5 | Employees experiment with AI; usage is uneven and often personal. | Move from informal experimentation to approved workflow adoption |

## People / skills evidence

The case shows capability in four areas:

- **Delegation:** Employees can identify repetitive work they would delegate.
- **Description:** Frontline SMEs can describe detailed workflow steps.
- **Discernment:** Staff distinguish judgment-heavy tasks from mechanical work.
- **Diligence:** Governance and accuracy concerns are explicit.

The gap is not willingness to experiment; it is controlled organizational practice.

## Self-report versus observed evidence

**Measured — B2:** Survey data shows AI use and concern.

**Measured — B4:** Shadowing shows manual workflow complexity that survey responses cannot capture.

The strongest discovery conclusions therefore combine self-report, observation and system artifacts rather than treating survey responses as direct measures of productivity.

## Adoption blockers

1. **Governance uncertainty:** 38% of survey respondents cited uncertainty about whether AI is allowed. Mechanism: employees either avoid useful work or use personal accounts.
2. **Privacy risk:** 27% cited privacy. Mechanism: PHI creates a hard boundary around general-purpose AI.
3. **Ownership:** Marcus explicitly identifies finding an owner as a problem. Mechanism: pilots can stall after technical feasibility.

The smallest removal is to define an accountable business owner and an approved pilot data-flow/review path before selecting technology.
