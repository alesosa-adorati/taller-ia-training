# Cross-Team Pattern — Information Transfer and Chasing

## Pattern

Three workflows share the same structural problem:

- **WF-O1 Prior authorization:** coordinators repeatedly chase status across payer channels.
- **WF-O2 Referral intake:** coordinators transfer information from fax documents into Caregate.
- **WF-R3 Appeals:** billing specialists search fragmented prior documentation for reusable evidence.

The common pattern is not simply “people use spreadsheets.” It is that information exists in one place or format while the next workflow requires a person to find, interpret and transfer it.

## Combined measurable scale

**Measured — B9:** PA requests are 2,400/month.

**Measured — B9:** Referrals are 5,800/month.

**Measured — B9:** Denials worked are 3,100/month.

These are not additive workload hours; they are separate workflow volumes.

## Why one capability could be cheaper

A shared pattern of document extraction, retrieval, status summarization, audit logging and human review could potentially reuse governance, evaluation and integration components.

That does not prove that one technical platform is cheaper. The case does not contain implementation-cost estimates.

## What differs

- PA requires payer status integration and escalation.
- Referral intake requires document extraction and validation.
- Appeals require secure retrieval and semantic matching against prior evidence.

The shared pattern is therefore a discovery hypothesis for reusable capability, not a recommendation to purchase one platform.
