# WF-R3 — Denial Appeal Preparation and Submission

## Owner and trigger

**Owner:** Revenue Cycle / denials and appeals.

**Trigger:** A denial is selected for appeal.

## Current-state steps

1. **System/human:** Receive denial from Caregate/ClearBridge.
2. **Human:** Determine denial category.
3. **Human:** Review claim and supporting information.
4. **Human:** Retrieve clinical documentation.
5. **Human:** Search old SharePoint appeal letters.
6. **Human:** Research payer policy language.
7. **Human:** Draft appeal letter.
8. **Human:** Assemble supporting documents.
9. **Human:** Submit through payer portal or fax.
10. **Human:** Wait for payer response.
11. **Human:** Record outcome and work follow-up.

## Timing

**Reported — B3:** One appeal example required approximately 20 minutes for the letter and 20 minutes for documents, or 40+ minutes total.

**Measured — B9:** 3,100 denials are worked per month.

**Measured — B9:** 34% of denials have missing/invalid PA as a denial category.

**Measured — B9:** 58% of appeals are overturned.

## Reusable knowledge

**Measured — B7:** The appeal folder contains approximately 1,400 documents.

**Reported — B3:** Nia uses old SharePoint letters as reference material.

## Human judgment boundary

Finding relevant prior appeals and assembling evidence are candidates for augmentation. Final appeal content, policy interpretation and submission approval should remain with a human.

## Key contradiction

The organization has a large body of prior appeal material and a structured denial report, yet the case does not show an indexed mechanism connecting denial categories to successful prior appeals.

That is an opportunity hypothesis, not proof of a productivity gap.

## Evidence limitations

The denial workflow was not shadowed. Timing and process detail are based on one interview and system artifacts.
