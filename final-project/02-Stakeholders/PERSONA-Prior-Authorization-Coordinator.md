# Persona — Prior Authorization Coordinator

**Representative:** Dorothy Kimball  
**Department:** Practice Operations  
**Workflow:** Prior authorization request processing and payer follow-up  
**Type:** Frontline SME / potential pilot user

## Goals

- Process authorization requests accurately.
- Reduce payer chasing.
- Surface meaningful changes without repeated manual checking.
- Retain judgment for exceptions.
- Preserve institutional knowledge.
- Be able to take time away without the workflow depending on one person.

## Current context

**Reported — B3:** There are 14 prior-authorization coordinators across sites.

**Measured — B4:** The observed tracker contained 431 rows, with 34 rows due for chase.

**Measured — B4:** One payer follow-up involved a 13-minute hold and 21 minutes total after a re-fax.

**Measured — B4:** Three observed new requests took 19, 14 and 17 minutes.

## Pain points

The dominant pain is repeated status chasing across payer portals, phone calls and fax. The workflow also relies heavily on a personal tracker.

**Reported — B3:** Dorothy checks the tracker even on holiday and is the person colleagues ask for answers.

**Reported — B3:** She would hand every chase to an assistant but retain clinical judgment.

## Judgment boundary

Administrative status chasing is a candidate for augmentation. Clinical or patient-impacting judgment should remain with a human.

**Reported — B8:** Dorothy wants something that performs chasing and tells her what changed so she can recover her afternoons.

## Adoption requirements

A useful solution must surface exceptions, preserve evidence, distinguish facts from inference, escalate ambiguous cases and operate within Kestrel's PHI governance requirements.

## Evidence limitation

This persona is based on one interview and one shadowing session and is not automatically representative of every coordinator.

**Sources:** B3, B4, B7, B8.
